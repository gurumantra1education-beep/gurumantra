GD and PI preparation when your percentile is low

Why the interview matters more at lower percentiles, and a practical plan to prepare for group discussions, personal interviews and WAT.

By the Guru Mantra team · 31 Oct 2026

At top institutes your CAT score does a lot of the work. At most other good B-schools, the group discussion (GD), personal interview (PI) and written ability test (WAT) often decide the final list. That's good news if your percentile is on the lower side: a strong interview can make up for a few percentile points.

What colleges are looking for

  • Clarity: can you explain why you want an MBA or PGDM, and why now?
  • Awareness: do you follow business and current affairs?
  • Communication: can you make a point clearly and listen to others?
  • Self-awareness: do you know your strengths, gaps and goals?

Personal interview: prepare these answers

  1. Tell me about yourself. Two minutes: background, what you've done, why management.
  2. Why an MBA/PGDM? Link your past to a specific goal. Avoid generic answers.
  3. Why this college? Mention something real: a specialisation, a recruiter, a course.
  4. Your low score. Be honest, don't make excuses, and show what you've done since.
  5. Your graduation subject. Expect basic questions from your own field.
  6. Work experience, if any: your role, one achievement, one lesson.

Practise out loud and record yourself. Most students sound very different from how they think they sound.

Group discussion: how to stand out

  • Read daily: one business newspaper and one current-affairs source.
  • Enter early, but with substance. One clear, relevant point beats three rushed ones.
  • Build on others ("Adding to that…") instead of only contradicting.
  • Summarise if the group allows it. It shows structure.
  • Stay calm. Shouting over others counts against you.

Written ability test (WAT)

You'll usually get a topic and 15–30 minutes. Use a simple structure: a short introduction with your stand, two or three arguments with examples, and a conclusion. Keep your handwriting or typing clean, and leave a minute to proofread.

A two-week plan

  • Days 1–3: write and rehearse your "tell me about yourself", "why MBA" and "why this college" answers.
  • Days 4–10: one mock GD and one mock PI every other day; read the news daily.
  • Days 11–14: practise two WAT essays and revise your graduation basics.
