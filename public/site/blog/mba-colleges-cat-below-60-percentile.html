How to read these cutoffs

A cutoff is the lowest percentile at which a college shortlisted students last year. Meeting it gets you a call for the next round, usually a group discussion (GD), personal interview (PI) or written ability test (WAT). It doesn't guarantee a seat.

Most colleges also weigh your academics, work experience and interview performance. At lower percentiles these often matter more than a few percentile points, so a strong interview can carry you.

Five routes when your CAT score is low

1. Colleges with published cutoffs at your level. Start with the table above and filter by the city and budget you're comfortable with.

2. MAT and CMAT. In our database, 794 colleges accept MAT and 624 accept CMAT. If you haven't written either exam, check the next available session. MAT runs several times a year. Read our MAT vs CMAT guide for the difference.

3. State exams. If you're open to studying in your home state, exams like MAH-CET, TANCET, Karnataka PGCET, AP ICET and TS ICET lead to state university MBAs that are often far cheaper.

4. Colleges without a published CAT cutoff. More than 1,000 colleges in our data accept CAT, MAT or CMAT but don't publish a fixed cutoff. They shortlist on your overall profile. Our CAT predictor lists these after the cutoff-based results.

5. Retaking CAT, but only if it fits your goals. A drop year makes sense if you're aiming for a specific top institute and have a clear plan to improve. It's a weaker choice if you'd be just as happy at a good college you can join this year.

Before you pay a fee anywhere

Check AICTE approval for a PGDM, or university affiliation for an MBA. Compare average placement packages with total fees, and ask how many students were actually placed. Our guide to choosing a B-school walks through these checks.
